01 · About

Scientist, engineer, translator between bench and business

My work sits across the bench and the pipeline: 5+ years spanning metabolic disease modeling, neurodegeneration, and the imaging-heavy workflows that turn raw data into biological answers, skills that map directly onto drug discovery, target validation, and preclinical development in biopharma.

Currently, at Gladstone Institutes

I'm deep-phenotyping transdifferentiated neurons ("t-neurons") derived directly from patient fibroblasts to study Parkinson's Disease, converting skin cells into neurons without passing through an iPSC stage. This preserves aging-associated epigenetic signatures that iPSC reprogramming tends to erase, making it a stronger model for late-onset neurodegeneration. Using a panel of live-cell biosensors, I track lysosomal function, α-synuclein turnover, and mitochondrial and neuronal health in these neurons over time by robotic microscopy.

Previously at UC San Diego, I characterized metabolic heterogeneity using advanced imaging and built automated MATLAB/ImageJ pipelines that improved analysis accuracy by 80%, while identifying disease mechanisms in Type 2 diabetes β-cell dysfunction and pancreatic cancer.

Before the bench, I spent three years in lab informatics product management, deploying LIMS/ELN systems for 30+ research institutions across three continents and owning regulatory compliance, workflow automation, and stakeholder alignment. That combination of rigorous experimental science plus scalable, product-driven execution is what I bring to fast-paced biopharma teams.
